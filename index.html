What We Do

Play With Pride organizes regular, accessible recreational sports sessions where queer youth and allies can participate without the pressure or judgment that can sometimes come with traditional competitive sports environments. Every session is designed to be low-barrier, welcoming and genuinely fun.

Why PlayWithPride?

PlayWithPride exists to create spaces where participants can show up as themselves and enjoy sport alongside other queer youth and supportive community members.

Our story

Why We Founded PlayWithPride

PlayWithPride was founded by a group of local Burnaby residents who remembered what it felt like to not see themselves in sport, to feel unwelcome in locker rooms, overlooked or judged by their peers, or simply too anxious to show up at all.

Our Focus

We believe sports and exercise are an incredible asset to everyone, and we want to ensure the queer youth of today have not only the opportunity to engage in judgment-free sports, but also a safe and welcoming community where they can thrive.

We started small: a run-club event at Burnaby Lake, open to any queer youth or ally who wanted to come out. That first session had three participants. Today, we've expanded to providing 100+ young people with opportunities to try out basketball, tennis, badminton, run clubs, weight training, soccer, and more.

LGBTQ2S+ youth may encounter additional barriers to participating in sports, such as discrimination, bullying, fear of judgment, lack of representation, or feeling uncomfortable in traditional sporting environments.

By the Numbers

The barriers are real. Research consistently shows that LGBTQ2S+ youth face significant barriers to sports participation, and that those barriers have real consequences for their health and wellbeing.

68%

of LGBTQ+ youth report feeling unsafe in sports environments

GLSEN National School Climate Survey, 2021
2×

more likely to be physically inactive compared to non-LGBTQ+ peers

Canadian Community Health Survey, 2019
45%

have experienced harassment or discrimination in a sports setting

Athlete Ally, Out on the Fields Study, 2015
3×

higher rates of depression and anxiety among LGBTQ+ youth who are excluded from sport

Journal of Adolescent Health, 2020

When queer youth have access to safe, welcoming sports spaces, the outcomes are transformative: improved mental health, stronger social connections, greater physical confidence, and a sense of belonging that extends far beyond the gym.

Our mission is to create inclusive sporting spaces where queer youth can participate, connect, and thrive.
